About Magic: The Gathering

Created by mathematician Richard Garfield and published by Wizards of the Coast in 1993, Magic: The Gathering invented the trading card game as a category. More than three decades later it supports a huge range of ways to play: Standard and Modern for competitive players, Draft and Sealed for people who love opening packs with purpose, and Commander — the 100-card social format that has become the way most Magic is actually played around kitchen tables and game stores.

That format depth is what makes Magic unique as a collecting hobby. A card that's unplayable in one format can be a staple in another, reprints and premium treatments give the same card many collectible versions, and the Reserved List and older sets anchor a serious vintage market. Universes Beyond crossovers have also pulled in collectors from far outside traditional Magic circles.

For newer players, a preconstructed Commander deck is the single best on-ramp into the game — you get a complete, playable deck and a collection seed in one box. Ask us if you want a recommendation.
